Conclusion & references

What the seven days showed, what it means for handling the fruit, and the sources that support the explanation.

4.1 · Conclusion

A single hormone reorganises the whole fruit

Over seven days at ambient room temperature, five mature-green Cavendish fingers completed the full climacteric ripening sequence without any added ethylene. Peel colour went from 1 to 5, the firmness score from 1 to 5 (hard to mushy) and sweetness from 1 to 5, while the pulp changed from hard, pale and starchy to very soft and very sweet. The changes were not simultaneous: colour moved first, texture followed, and flavour was the last attribute to reach the top of the scale.

That sequence is the observable expression of a single molecular event — the autocatalytic burst of ethylene at the onset of the climacteric. Ethylene initiates chlorophyll breakdown, cell-wall disassembly, starch hydrolysis and volatile synthesis in a coordinated programme, and the fact that the changes are staggered simply reflects how long each downstream process takes to run. Ripening is therefore best understood not as decay but as a programmed developmental stage that happens to occur after harvest.

Key findings

  • Colour broke on Day 2 and the peel was fully yellow by Day 5.
  • Colour held for two days (Days 2–3) before the autocatalytic phase.
  • Softening accelerated at the end of the week, reaching mushy on Day 7.
  • Sweetness scored 1 → 3 → 5 on Days 1, 4 and 7.
  • The edible window closed by Day 7, when brown spots appeared.

Implications for postharvest handling

Harvest

Pick at the right index

Fruit harvested at index 1–2 has the full ripening capacity documented here. Fruit left to break colour on the plant arrives with a shortened shelf life, because the climacteric has already started.

Storage

Slow the clock with temperature

Every ripening process observed here is temperature-dependent. Holding fruit a few degrees cooler lengthens the window substantially — but below about 13 °C banana suffers chilling injury, so the margin is narrow.

Distribution

Manage ethylene, not just time

Because ethylene is autocatalytic and gaseous, one ripe fruit accelerates the whole consignment. Segregation, ventilation, ethylene scrubbing or 1-MCP treatment all interrupt the loop at a different point.

Limitation of the conclusion

These findings describe five fingers from one bunch under uncontrolled room conditions. They illustrate the general climacteric pattern well, but the exact day on which each stage is reached will differ with cultivar, harvest maturity, temperature and handling history. The experiment would need replication across several bunches to support a quantitative claim.
