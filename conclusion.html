6 · Conclusion

One hormone, three visible changes

Over seven days of storage, six banana samples from a single stem passed through the full ripening sequence without any added ethylene. Peel colour went from 1 to 5, firmness from 1 to 5 as the fruit softened from very hard to mushy, and sweetness from 1 to 5, from astringent and starchy to very sweet. Brown senescent spotting appeared on the final day, marking the end of the edible window.

The changes were not simultaneous. Colour ran ahead of firmness, and sweetness followed both. That stagger is exactly what the physiology predicts: each attribute is the output of a different enzyme system — chlorophyllase for colour, hydrolytic enzymes for starch conversion, pectinases and cellulases for texture — all switched on by the same autocatalytic ethylene signal but each running at its own rate.

The lag phase on Days 1–2 and the rapid change from Day 3 onward confirm the climacteric pattern described by Tipu and Sherif (2024): a period of basal ethylene followed by an autocatalytic rise. For handling, the practical conclusion is that ripeness cannot be judged from one attribute alone, and that the window between “ready to sell” and “past its best” is short — roughly the difference between Day 5 and Day 7 in this study.

Key findings

  • Colour broke on Day 2; the peel was fully yellow by Days 5–6.
  • Firmness held until Day 3, then fell rapidly through Days 4–7.
  • Sweetness scored 1 → 3 → 5 on Days 1, 4 and 7.
  • Brown senescent spotting appeared on Day 7.
  • Colour advanced ahead of firmness throughout.

Implications for postharvest handling

Harvest

Harvest at the green stage

Fruit cut at score 1 has the full ripening capacity demonstrated here. Fruit left to colour on the plant arrives with a shortened shelf life, because the climacteric has already begun.

Storage

Slow the clock with temperature

Every process observed here is temperature-dependent. Holding fruit cooler lengthens the window — but below about 13 °C banana suffers chilling injury, so the margin is narrow.

Distribution

Manage ethylene, not just time

Because ethylene is autocatalytic and gaseous, one ripening fruit accelerates the whole consignment. Segregation, ventilation or ethylene scrubbing each interrupt the loop at a different point.

Scope of these conclusions

These findings describe six bananas from one stem, held at room conditions and scored on a five-point scale. They illustrate the climacteric pattern clearly, but the exact day on which each stage is reached will differ with cultivar, harvest maturity, temperature and handling history.
