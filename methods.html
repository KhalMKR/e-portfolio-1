Objectives, materials & method

What the experiment set out to show, what was used, and how the samples were handled over the seven days of storage.

2 · Objectives

Three aims

  1. To observe the changes in banana ripening

    Across a seven-day storage period, following the same samples from Day 1 to Day 7.

  2. To evaluate changes in peel colour, firmness and sweetness

    Using a standardised scoring scale of 1 to 5 applied consistently each day.

  3. To identify the relationship between ripening, firmness reduction and increasing sweetness

    Testing whether softening and sweetening advance together during storage.

Experimental summary

  • Samples6 bananas, one stem
  • Duration7 days
  • ParametersColour, firmness, sweetness
  • Scale1 to 5
  • ConditionStored at room conditions

3.1 · Materials

Materials used

  1. Fresh banana

    One stem, from which six samples were taken.

  2. Labels or stickers

    To identify each sample individually throughout the storage period.

  3. Marker pen

    For writing the sample codes on the labels and recording daily scores.

  4. Smartphone or camera

    For the daily photographic record of physical appearance.

  5. Knife

    To open a sample when sweetness had to be assessed by tasting.

  6. Observation and recording sheets

    For the daily scores of colour, firmness and sweetness.

3.2 · Method

How the samples were handled

  1. Select and label the samples

    Six banana samples were selected from one stem and labelled individually for identification.

  2. Store the samples

    The bananas were then stored and observed over a period of seven days.

  3. Keep conditions constant

    The samples were kept together at room conditions and left undisturbed between daily observations, so that the only variable was time.

  4. Observe daily

    Daily observations were conducted to monitor changes in the bananas throughout the ripening process.

  5. Photograph each day

    Photos were taken each day to record the physical appearance of the samples.

  6. Score the three parameters

    Three main parameters were evaluated: peel colour, firmness and sweetness, each on the 1–5 scale defined in Table 1.

A note on tasting

Colour and firmness can be judged without damaging the fruit, but sweetness cannot: it requires cutting a sample open. That is why sweetness was scored only on the days a sample was opened — Days 1, 4 and 7 — while colour and firmness were scored every day. These gaps are marked “–” in Table 2.
